The Witness Plane

Core ⇄ runner ⇄ the outside world. Third companion to destination.html and architecture.html, and the part they left blank — which is awkward, because it is the busiest module in the repo and it is where the deterministic floor gets its evidence.

An integration is not a feature. It is how the kernel learns a fact it did not produce — and the only source of evidence no model wrote.

1 · Four roles, not eight vendors

Today a provider declares mechanism: canDispatch, canReceiveWebhook, canDeploy, multiBinding. That is the right shape for routing and the wrong shape for deciding whether its output can back a claim. The destination declares role in the fact model alongside it.

4 · reverify — the capability that makes the reconciler possible

The architecture document's sole-witness rule says every box-observed fact carries an externally re-queryable witness id. Nothing today can re-query one. Health checks answer "is this provider up"; the status service answers "what is this binding's state". Neither answers "is fact F still true in the world".

The three-valued answer is the whole point. A binary reverify turns every provider outage into a wave of false corrections.

5 · Who may touch the world

The box may act on the world only where the act witnesses itself.
A push produces a SHA anyone can re-query. A deploy, a merge, a message, a query — all go through core, because core holds the credential and core is what has to record the witness.
And the consequence for direct-mcp is not "remove it". It is: a fact whose provenance is a direct-MCP call is agent-signed, carries the weakest independence in §2, and may never alone satisfy a kernel claim — no matter that a real provider answered. The credential being real does not make the report independent; the agent chose what to ask and what to report back.
