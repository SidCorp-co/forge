1 · Service map

Fourteen services, five planes. The four in purple on the top two planes are what makes the rest mechanical rather than aspirational.

2 · A fact lands

The gate runs at insert, not in the projection. Checking later means the bad fact is already in the log.

3 · A question, across services

Only one thing here is ordinary product work. The dedup/coalesce box, the auto-answer citation, and the premise write have no published prior art — instrument the false-auto-answer rate from day one.

4 · Scope blocking — what an overturned answer reaches

Revision 1 called this an exact set derived from agent-declared premises. Refuted — the set was a lower bound labelled as exact, and the declaration was unverifiable self-report. What the kernel has instead is what it served, and the blocking unit is the question's scope.

The default is block. The bucket is named outside the answer's scope, never "no record of reading it" — absence of a record is not evidence of safety, and an exemption is a priced amnesty with a named end condition rather than a computation. Until the compensation is paid the projection reads shipped_with_open_compensation, not shipped.

5 · Custody chain of a merge — and the floor beside it

Revision 1 called this a proof chain. Refuted — it checks shape, not substance, and only two of its seven links are load-bearing. It stays, priced as audit; the substance lives in the floor below it.

6 · Demotion ratchet — autonomy is lost automatically, never granted

Revision 1 had a closed loop that raised and lowered a trust budget. Refuted — granting autonomy is the act of not checking, so the loop destroys the label supply that feeds it. At this project's volume one cell needs about 41 years.

8 · Three artifacts, three clocks

The slow clock is uneven on purpose: boxes are machines people own, and they update when they update. Anything whose correctness depends on the fleet being current is already broken.

13 · The clocks, measured

The fast clock may never outrun the slow one.
A policy version declaring a predicate this core build cannot evaluate is refused at registration, naming the predicate; the box keeps the last admitted version. Revision 1 said "no change may require two clocks", which is unachievable: splitting declare from enforce across repos is itself a two-clock generator.

And one migration rule falls out of it. A migration backing a fact type referenced by any admitted policy version is forward-only. Roll it back and you destroy the only interpreter for facts that in-flight sessions are still writing under their pinned version. Which means the capability manifest above is not optional — it is what tells you which migrations are pinned by live policy.
