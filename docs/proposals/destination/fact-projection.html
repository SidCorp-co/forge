Fact & Projection

Facts are written and never touched again. Everything a screen shows is computed from them and has no writer at all.

1 · One fact

2 · Two clocks

Two timestamp columns buy most of bitemporality. Full FOR SYSTEM_TIME AS OF semantics cost a different storage engine and answer a question nobody here has asked yet.

3 · Correction is a new row

4 · Three doors, all at INSERT

Gate 1 has a write-skew hole. Close it on purpose. "No verdict whose author is the author of the code in this subtree" is a recursive read under contention, and a naive EXISTS lets two concurrent inserts both pass. Pick one: SERIALIZABLE, an explicit lock on the root row, or a denormalised root_id + author on every fact. Not choosing is choosing the broken one.

5 · The projection has no writer

This is what answers the standard objection to facts-plus-projections — that a projection lags, while a dispatcher needs current status to decide what to claim.

6 · Changing f() over history

The facts are the truth; a projection is one opinion about them, and it is replaceable.
Which is why policy_version is stamped on the fact: it tells f() what the words meant when they were written, so a vocabulary change does not silently rewrite history.

7 · What the kinds are for

A fact's kind is what a claim class points at, so the taxonomy is not decoration — it decides what evidence is demanded.
