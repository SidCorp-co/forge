The Session Tree

One table for work. Every row says which species it is and who owns it, and closure follows the owner edge rather than a timer per species.

1 · One row

2 · Liveness is inherited; observation is not

A child runs inside its parent's process, so it cannot outlive the parent. Silence from the root means every node beneath it is already gone — no per-node heartbeat is needed, and none is kept.

3 · Closing — one behaviour, and the rule for earning a second

The subtree closes because the edge exists, not because each species carries its own timer. That is the whole reason parent_id is a column with a foreign key rather than something the box reports.

4 · Both directions, or it leaks

The last clause matters: a parent whose final child failed may never conclude completed. Closing on "all children terminal" without reading the last outcome is how a failed tree reports success.

5 · Held is a fourth shape, and it is not an orphan

A held node is not running and is not finished. The session process is gone and the answer starts a fresh one — the row is what persists, never the pane.

6 · Three caps, three different refusals

CapBoundsThe refusal must name
depthhow deep a tree may gothe depth reached and the limit — a subagent spawning subagents forever is a runaway with no other symptom
concurrencylive children at oncewho currently holds the slots, so an operator can see what to stop
budgetspend under a rootthat the tree was stopped by budget, distinctly from failing — the two look identical downstream otherwise
A cap bounds the tree. It is not a liveness edge.
Hitting a cap closes nodes by the same rules as anything else, and writes a fact saying which cap fired.

7 · Adopt — the word that means two things

AdoptingAllowed?Why
a pane left by a dead daemonyesthe pane is plumbing; finding it again and resuming its conversation loses nothing
a session row left by a dead masterneverits children died with it, so every open row beneath it is a corpse. Adopting one means reporting work nobody is doing
Declare dead, then take new work — in that order. A claim that lands between the two reads looks exactly like a job that just died. Inverting them is how a fresh master adopts corpses as live work.

Honest costs

CostWhat it buys, and who pays
A species column is five call sites and a backfillPaid in ISS-1136. The backfill infers from the run's metadata, the job link and the run kind, once and frozen; every branch needs a positive signal, and a row with none aborts the migration naming its id rather than taking a default that would read afterwards as a fact
A foreign key on parent_id can refuse a write that works todayPaid in ISS-1136, and the paths were enumerated: one writer, applyRunLedgerSnapshot, reached over the runner:sessions frame. It refuses the edge by name before the constraint sees it, so a bad claim costs an operator one edge rather than the whole snapshot
Merging two timeouts changes when work is reclaimedPaid in ISS-1136, at the longer number. A hold returned late costs a delay; a lease returned early costs two boxes on one issue, and only the second makes the state lie. A dead master's holds now sit in the pool for up to ten minutes rather than three, and the condition that would end the merge is a measurement of how long a healthy master's heartbeat goes quiet under load
Withdrawing the three close policies loses a shape that was easy to write aboutTemporal's vocabulary reads well and Forge does not have the behaviours behind it. The page is less impressive and more true
