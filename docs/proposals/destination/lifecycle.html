Intent Lifecycle

From what someone wants to a thing that is done, cancelled, or still owed — and who is allowed to hold it while that happens.

1 · The whole life

done and cancelled are both terminal and must never collapse into one. A projection that counts them together cannot tell delivered work from abandoned work, and every throughput number built on it is wrong in the flattering direction.

2 · An Intent is versioned, and a running attempt keeps the revision it started on

3 · Order between Intents

RuleDetail
An edge gates admission, not executiona blocked Intent never becomes claimable; nothing has to stop work that already started, because it could not have started
The edge is the only thing that gatesprose in a description gates nothing — if the order matters it is an edge or it does not exist
A cycle is refused at write timenaming both ends. A cycle detected later is a deadlock nobody can see
An edge is created with the Intent, atomicallyadmitting first and adding the edge after leaves a window where the work is claimable — and something will claim it
A blocker that is cancelled does not releaseonly done releases. Cancelling a prerequisite and having its dependents start is how abandoned work turns into shipped work

4 · What comes next, and why that is policy

5 · The lease — one holder, fleet-wide

"One master per (runner, project)" bounds a box. It does not bound a fleet — two boxes serving one project is the ordinary case, and the lease is the only thing standing between them.

6 · Attempts

An attempt is the unit the harness counts.
Escape rate, intervention rate and verdict accuracy are all per-attempt. Without an attempt number in the model, every one of those numbers is measured on something undefined.
