agent-execution — a claimed job becomes a running agent

One drive job on a runner box, from the master that decides to take it to the moment a claude process is alive — and every exit that kills it on the way. Owner: packages/runner/crates/forge-runner-core/src/daemon/{master,control,terminal,dispatch}.rs, packages/core/src/devices/{claim,master-session,master-reaper}.ts.

Who takes the job, and where that decision lives

The master is a resident tmux session named forge-master-<slug>, not a print process the daemon spawns per pass. Its identity is its agent_sessions row — the same uuid jobs.held_by carries — and taking a job is two acts, not one: prepare takes the hold and hands back a job token with no process started; start stamps the dispatch, and that stamp is what ends the hold.

The agent stays a daemon child on purpose. A tmux pane would cost the structured stdout job_events is parsed from, and job_events?sinceSeq is the exact signal the master needs to see an agent that is alive and not progressing. So an agent is named and addressable (forge-runner agent list|send, the session inbox and its ack) rather than pane-hosted — the price paid, and what it buys.

The rule the shape encodes

Every verdict on this page is matched above the cc-startup signal branch in classifyKind, and that is not a detail. Each of these jobs died before a process existed, and the pre-spawn heartbeat above posts a job event every 25s — which is all deriveCcStartupSignals needs to call it a startup death. A verdict added below that branch is unreachable, not merely late. A new failure on this path goes above it.

No unbounded wait while holding a lock another job needs. The root lock is per-repo; the permit ceiling is per-box. Nesting them made one project's claims kill another project's queue, and the error named the lock (ISS-920: 7 repo_lock_timeout in 30 minutes on codemap, while forge-dev held two of three permits).

Two properties keep it un-nested, and both are load-bearing:

  • The lock band is a lexical block in dispatch::handle. The compiler releases it, not a drop call somebody can move.
  • ClaudeCodeRunner::start touches nothing in the root. Putting root work back inside it re-nests the lock around the permit wait, whatever the block looks like.

The permit wait is bounded too, at one residency window — but a bound is not what makes this safe. For the whole of a bound the lock would still be held and the siblings would still die, just sooner.

The stamp ends the hold, in one statement. Past start the job is not queued and no release path can reach it. That is why no release may ever unwind a dispatch stamp — one that did re-queued two live agents' jobs underneath them (epodsystem, 2026-09-05). It is also the bound on B3: a master that dies between reading the pool and claiming parks nothing, because a claim that succeeds has already ended its own hold.

The master writes nothing in the checkout it stands in. It holds the base branch; every agent it starts works in a worktree cut from origin/<base>. That is what makes a detached master safe here where a detached drive agent would not be — do not weaken it to make anything else easier.

Read this before

Changing what dispatch::handle does under the lock, moving anything into or out of ClaudeCodeRunner::start, or touching REPO_LOCK_WAIT / SESSION_PERMIT_WAIT / PRE_SPAWN_BEAT_BUDGET — those three are derived from each other and the build fails if the ordering breaks.
