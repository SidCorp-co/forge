What happens when it goes wrong

FailureWho noticesWhat is done about it
Master pane vanishes (crash, tmux kill-session, box reboot) The daemon, next sweep (≤30s) — master.rs::supervise POST /me/pool/release with no jobId returns every hold that session had, then /me/master-session/close ends the row. The next sweep starts a fresh master. Nothing it had already started is affected: a started job holds no hold.
Master alive but prints nothing after a prompt The daemon, at MASTER_SILENCE_CEILING (10 min of transcript with no growth) Kill the pane, then the same release-and-close path. A master that is idle without having been prompted is not touched — residency is legal, silence-after-asking is not.
The whole box vanishes (power, network partition) Core, at MASTER_HOLD_TIMEOUT_MS (3 min) reapDeadMasterHolds sweeps every minute and judges by the session heartbeat. This arm is the only one that works when nothing local is left to notice.
prepare succeeds and start never comes The daemon, at PREPARE_TTL (2 min), then core's reaper at 3 control::reap_preparations releases the hold. The two bounds are ordered on purpose: the process that took the job is the one that hands it back, and the reaper is the backstop for the process itself dying.
start arrives after the reaper took the hold Core, in the stamp's WHERE Refused hold_lost; the daemon drops the spawn. The job stays plain queued and claimable — never stamped onto a box with no process on it.
Agent alive and not progressing The master, reading job_events?sinceSeq Nothing automatic. The master decides and reports; core decides retry. This is the one detection that has no daemon-side arm, which is why the agent keeps its structured stdout and does not get a tmux pane of its own.
Runner build too old to name its agent Core, at prepare Refused runner_too_old before any hold is taken. /me/pool/claim, the pre-ISS-919 one-shot verb, answers the same thing to every caller — it is a named refusal rather than a second live path.

The two rules a reader most often gets wrong

The stamp ends the hold, in one statement. Past start, the job is not queued and no release can reach it. That is why no release path may ever unwind a dispatch stamp — one that did re-queued two live agents' jobs underneath them (epodsystem, 2026-09-05).
The master writes nothing in the checkout it stands in. It holds the base branch; every agent works in a worktree cut from origin/<base>. That is what makes a detached master safe here where a detached drive agent would not be.
