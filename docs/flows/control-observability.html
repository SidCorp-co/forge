How a fault reaches a person, and how work becomes a number

a hop with nobody at the end is not a hop

1A fault on a box travels until it reaches somebody who can act

flowchart TD
  F["something fails on a box"] --> R1["the box records it with what it was doing"]
  R1 --> R2["the control plane keeps it, and shows it
on the screen the work belongs to"]
  R2 --> Q{"is anybody watching that screen?"}
  Q -->|"yes"| OK["it is acted on"]
  Q -->|"no"| N["it sits there. Nothing pushes it further —
no alert, no message, no page"]

Faults are pulled, not pushed. That is a deliberate limit rather than a gap to be filled quietly: a fault nobody reads is indistinguishable from no fault, so anyone relying on this to notice an outage must actually be looking. Where that is not good enough, the answer is an external watcher, not a louder record.

The measure exists to be embarrassing. A fix applied by hand and not counted is the one that never gets designed out, so repairs made directly in the database are exactly the ones that must show up here.

What is measured

Numberwhat it answers
Interventions per issuehow much hand-holding the machine still needs
Request → running workhow long from asking to something happening
Step durationswhere time actually goes
Failures by causewhat breaks, and whether it is us or the box

The first two are the ones the design is steered by. The rest explain them.

A chart groups rows by time, and the grouping must be computed once

Turning timestamped rows into points on a chart means deciding which bucket each row falls in. That decision is made in two places — where the rows are queried and where the chart is drawn — and if the two disagree, points land in the wrong bucket or vanish.

Nothing about this fails loudly: a chart with a wrong bucket looks like a chart. The rule is that the two implementations are checked against each other rather than trusted to match, because the only symptom is a number that is quietly wrong.

When it goes wrong

What you seewhat is happeningwhat to do
An outage nobody noticed for hours. Working as designed — faults are pulled. Nothing was watching. Do not expect a louder record to fix it. Put an external watcher on the thing that must not go down.
The intervention count looks too good. Repairs are being made in a way the count cannot see — most often directly against the database. Route repairs through a path that records them. An uncounted fix is a design problem that never surfaces.
A chart is missing recent activity. Rows are landing in a bucket the chart does not draw, usually a time-zone or boundary disagreement between the query and the display. Compare the two bucket calculations directly. Do not assume the data is missing — it is usually mis-filed.
