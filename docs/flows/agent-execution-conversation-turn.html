A conversation turn, on any channel

Somebody says something in a room, and something answers as the project. This is what stands between the two, who owns each step, and which of the ways it can end is not a failure. Owners: packages/core/src/conversations/ports.ts (what a channel must supply), packages/core/src/conversations/turn-runner.ts (the turn itself), packages/core/src/conversations/screened-reply.ts (the screen and its fallbacks), packages/core/src/messaging/doors.ts (the doors and what each may repair), packages/core/src/conversations/transport-free.test.ts (the gate that keeps the turn free of any one channel).

A channel supplies four answers and nothing more, and every sentence that reaches a person leaves by one door. Which venue this is, who spoke, how to post, how to read back — with those four, the turn is the same turn on every channel. A channel that also carried its own turn would carry its own screen with it, and a rule tightened in one place would quietly stop holding in the other. The gate counts the channels that reach past the four: it must read zero.

The four ways a turn ends, and which of them is a fault

EndingWhat a reader should concludeWhose move is next
a sentence was posted The person read it, and the transcript holds exactly what they read together with the channel's own receipt for it. Those are different claims: the row proves we composed it, the receipt proves the venue took it. Nobody's. The turn is over.
not yet known Not a failure. The channel handed this turn to a worker that answers minutes later by its own path. A second sentence posted here would be the same answer twice. The worker's. Its own completion posts the answer.
nothing could be delivered The venue could not be reached under the project this turn ran for — most often because the room was re-pointed at a different project while the turn was thinking. Nothing is written to the transcript, because a row for a sentence nobody saw says the opposite of the truth. An operator's, if it repeats: the binding is the thing that moved.
refused to the speaker We could not tell who was speaking, so there was no identity to answer under. The refusal is the answer, and it names the step that fixes it. No transcript is opened. The speaker's — they link their own account; nobody does it for them.

What the rule costs, and why it is worth it

The screen may ask the writer to try again, and then it must stop. Each retry is a full turn of the project's own model, and somebody is waiting in the room while it runs. A writer that failed the check twice does not converge on a third; what it gets instead is a sentence we wrote ourselves, which says plainly that the answer could not be stood behind. How many retries a given door allows is a property of the door, not of the channel — a door where nobody is left to ask again allows none, and says so.

Measured when the turn was taken out of the first channel (2026-09-14): the channel's own connection file fell from 815 lines to 510, and the count of channel files reaching past the four answers went from three to zero. A second channel is now the four answers and no turn of its own.
