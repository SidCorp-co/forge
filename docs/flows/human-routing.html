flows / human-routing

How work reaches a person, and how their answer gets it moving again

a deliberate pause is not a cry for help · a question goes to where the people are

1One piece of work, and every point a person is in it

sequenceDiagram
  autonumber
  actor P as a person
  participant F as Forge
  participant M as whoever serves the project
  participant W as a worker

  P->>F: files a piece of work
  Note over F: work a WORKER filed starts as a draft. Nothing runs it,
and it reaches nobody except a list of drafts no person
has answered yet — so filing is not handing it over
  P->>F: releases it to be worked
  F->>M: offered, alongside everything else nobody has started
  M->>W: opens a run over it
  W->>F: says it has started

  alt a machine is in the way
    F-->>F: the attempt waits and is tried again.
Nobody is asked, and the work keeps its place
  else only a person can answer
    W->>F: stops, and must say why in its own words
    F->>P: on their list, and posted to the project's room
    P->>F: answers
    F->>W: to the same worker if it is still alive,
otherwise the work is picked up fresh
  end

  W->>F: finishes, and closes it
  F->>P: told that it shipped
  opt the person disagrees with that close
    P->>F: rejects it back for rework, saying why
    F->>M: offered again, exactly as unstarted work is
    Note over F,M: only where the project admits rework to be picked up.
Where it does not, the work sits until a person takes it by hand
  end

A person is in this exactly four times: releasing it, answering a question, being told it shipped, and disagreeing with that. Rework is the one that is commonly got wrong. Rejecting a close is a judgement only a person makes, but the work that follows it is ordinary work — so a project may put it straight back in front of whoever is serving it, and this one does. Nothing automatic starts it, and something automatic will happily take it.

The distinction carries the whole value of the list. Once a deliberate pause shows up as something demanding attention, the list stops being read, and then the real questions sit in it unanswered too.

3A question outlives the worker that asked it

flowchart TD
  A["a worker asks a question and stops"] --> B{"is that worker still alive?"}
  B -->|"yes"| C["the answer goes straight back to it,
with everything it already knew"]
  B -->|"no — it timed out or the box died"| D["the answer is recorded on the issue,
and the work is started fresh
by whoever picks it up next"]
  D --> E["so an answer is never lost —
but the context that raised it can be"]
  A2["an agent that is not running on a box
asks the same question"] --> D2["nothing is waiting to be woken,
so it reads the answer back itself
whenever it next looks"]

This is why answering quickly is worth something concrete: the same worker resumes with its own reasoning intact, instead of a new one rebuilding it from the issue. Nobody is penalised for a slow answer beyond that, and no question is dropped for it.

The second asker is the newer one, and it is the same question either way — same choices, same advised one, same room, same reply. What it does not have is a live worker holding the context, so nothing is woken for it and nothing is lost when nobody is: it comes back and looks.

4The question goes to where the people are

flowchart TD
  A["a question is open and a person is owed it"] --> B{"has this project been
given a chat room?"}
  B -->|"no"| X["nobody is told. Whoever can give it a room
is warned that a decision is waiting
with nowhere to go. Giving it a room later
delivers the same question —
the work is never made to ask twice"]
  B -->|"yes"| C["the question is posted to that room:
what is being decided, every choice,
which one is advised, and what
happens if nobody replies"]
  C --> D["replying in that conversation
with the number of a choice
is answering it"]
  D --> E{"does the reply say which choice,
and is the replier known to us?"}
  E -->|"yes, and they are allowed that choice"| F["it is recorded as their decision
and the stopped work wakes up"]
  E -->|"no"| G["they are told exactly what was wrong
in the same conversation, and asked again.
Nothing is assumed on their behalf"]
  C --> H["further rounds go in the same
conversation — one decision, one thread"]

The four things that get a reply turned down are each said by name: we do not know who you are on this chat server, that choice is not yours to make, you are answering a round that has been overtaken, and you did not name a choice. None of them guesses, and in particular none of them falls back to the advised choice — the advice exists to save a reader time, not to answer for someone who did not. A person who replies with something we cannot read gets the list of choices back, and the work stays stopped until they pick one.

5The comments on a piece of work and the room are one conversation

flowchart TD
  A["somebody writes a comment on a piece of work"] --> B{"has this project been

  B -->|"no"| X["it stays owed. Nothing is dropped and
nothing expires — give the project a room
later and the comment is carried then"]
  B -->|"yes"| C["the room is opened a conversation
for that piece of work, named after it,
and the comment is put inside it"]
  C --> D["every later comment goes in the
same conversation — one piece of work,
one thread"]
  D --> E["replying there writes a comment
on the work, as the person who typed it"]
  E --> F{"do we know who that is
on this chat server?"}
  F -->|"no"| G["they are told so by name, and told how to
link themselves. Nothing is written as the bot"]
  F -->|"yes"| H["the comment is theirs, and if the work was
stopped waiting on a person, it wakes up —
by the same route a comment typed in Forge takes"]
  C --> Y["a comment that came from the room
is never carried back into it"]

The two conversations a room may hold about one piece of work are kept apart on purpose: the one where a choice is being decided, and the one where people are talking. A chat thread cannot be told apart from the message alone otherwise, and "is this reply a decision or a remark?" has to be answerable before anything acts on it. So prose never becomes a decision — someone who types an opinion into the talking thread has not chosen an option, and the work that is waiting on a choice stays waiting. The same message arriving twice, which chat servers do, writes one comment and not two; a comment that could not be carried is carried later rather than forgotten, and the price of that promise is that a crash can put the same comment in the room twice. It is the right way round: a repeated sentence is visible, a lost one is not.

Who is owed what

Statewho acts
A question is openthe person it was asked of
Deliberately pausedwhoever paused it, when they choose
Waiting to go livea person, deciding to release
Rejected back for reworka person says why — then whoever serves the project, where it admits rework

Nothing automatic starts work in any of these four. Three of them stay that way until a person acts. Rework is the exception and it is the project's own choice: a project may offer rejected work to be picked up exactly as it offers work nobody has started, and a project that does not leaves it sitting. Everything else is somebody's turn in a queue that keeps turning.

Routing is by state, not by expertise

Work reaches a person because of the state it is in, not because anybody decided they were the right person for it. There is no routing by skill, ownership area, or authority — the list is the same list for everybody who can see the project.

That is a real limitation and it is stated here rather than implied: on a project with several people, deciding who takes which question is a conversation those people have, and nothing in the system helps them have it.

"Everybody who can see the project" is the whole of it, and it is two questions rather than one. Being the person a question is addressed to — the assignee, or whoever filed an issue nobody else owns — is what puts a row in front of you. Holding a role on the project it belongs to is what permits it, and losing that role takes the row away: leaving the project, or leaving the organisation that owns it, empties those rows out of your list. A row you may not see is absent from it rather than shown blanked, because a placeholder still says the issue exists. Read-only members are owed their questions like anybody else, and an organisation's owners and admins reach every project of theirs whether or not they were added to it by name.

Asked of one question at a time, that is: is this mine to answer, and may I see the project it is asked about? The list owed both and asked only the first until ISS-989.

When it goes wrong

What you seewhat is happeningwhat to do
The attention list is full of things nobody needs to act on. Deliberate pauses are being counted as needing attention. Treat it as a defect, not as noise to live with — a list nobody trusts is a list nobody reads.
A question was answered but nothing restarted. The answer landed after the worker was gone, so it is recorded on the issue and waiting to be picked up rather than resuming by itself. Put the work back in the queue. Check it went back to the stop it was at, not to the front.
A question nobody ever answers. It was posted to the project's room and read by nobody. Nothing escalates it beyond that first posting: it stays open and the work stays stopped. Answer it or abandon the work explicitly. There is no third outcome and nothing will force one.
Work is stopped on a question and the room was never told. The project has no chat room, so there was nowhere to post it. A warning went to whoever can give the project one. Give the project a room. The waiting question is posted on the next sweep — the work does not have to ask again, and no second decision is created.
Work was rejected back for rework and then nothing happened to it. Rejecting a close never starts anything by itself. Whether the work is offered to be picked up again is the project's own setting, and a project that has not said so leaves it sitting. Check whether this project admits rejected work to be picked up. If it does not and you want it to, that is a setting, not a defect — and until then somebody has to take it by hand.
A reply in the room was turned down and the person does not know why. This should be impossible: every refusal is answered in the same conversation, naming which of the four rules it broke. Treat a silent refusal as a defect. A reply that gets no response at all means the conversation was not recognised as a question's, and the message was handled as ordinary chat.
