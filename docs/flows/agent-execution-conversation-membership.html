Changing who is in a room

A room used to be whatever opened it. Measured on staging on 2026-09-14, all 74 rooms were one person and one project's agent, every one of them opened by a message arriving rather than by anybody choosing. A person could talk, and could do nothing at all about who was listening. This is what they can do now, and what each act costs — because two of these acts cannot be taken back, and the screen has to say so before it does them rather than after.

A room's projects are read from the agents in it. Nobody chooses them. No screen offers a picker, because there is nothing to pick: the room is about whatever its agents are about, and the only way to change that is to change which agents are in it. Every screen that prints the set says where it was read from, so a person who wants a different answer knows which lever to reach for.

The two acts, and what each one changes

They may not share one control. One list holding both makes a change to what the room can see look like an invitation. They are two buttons, two dialogues and two different sentences, and in the list of members afterwards an agent and a person are told apart at a glance rather than on inspection — different outline, different fill, different mark. The bar for "at a glance" is the 3:1 non-text contrast ratio, which is a number rather than an opinion.

What the confirmation has to say, and what it may never say

Adding an agent to a room that is already talking is not undone by removing it later. The confirmation says three things that are all true of what actually happens, and is forbidden one thing that is not:

It saysBecause
the agent is shown what has already been said here, not only what is said after it joins a turn reads its room back with no regard for when anybody joined it. There is no join-time cut anywhere: the newest part of the room goes to the model whoever is in it.
it reads the recent part of the room, not the whole of it what reaches the model is bounded by a window over the stored room, so a long room is not disclosed end to end. Saying "everything" would be the opposite error to saying "nothing".
removing it later stops it reading from that moment, and unreads nothing the reading already happened, and the replies it made are in the room where everybody present has already seen them.
who in the room today will lose it, by name, where adding this agent would cost anybody their access a room is readable only by somebody holding a role on every project in it, so bringing a second project in puts the room outside anybody who holds none on that one. They are not told, and nothing else on the screen says it will happen. The names are computed per candidate, because "somebody may lose access" is a warning nobody can act on.
never: "you can take it out again to undo this" that sentence is false, and a false reassurance in front of an irreversible act is worse than no confirmation at all.
The confirmation before a room is opened says less, because less is true. Nothing has been said in a room that does not exist, so the pre-join sentence would be a claim about nothing. What survives is what the room will be: its projects, whether it can be spoken in at all, and who will be able to read it.

Who may change a membership

Somebody already in the room, who could write in it. Two different questions, and both are asked. Being able to read a room is not permission to change who else can: a room shared across a project is readable by everyone holding a role on that project, and if reading were the bar any of them could quietly widen what it can see. So the person doing it must hold at least a working role on every project the room is about and be one of its own people.

A room whose people were never recorded — a channel room, where the conversation lives somewhere else and Forge only mirrors it — therefore has no membership anybody can change from here. That is the honest answer rather than a gap: who is in that room is decided where that room lives, and writing a membership here that the channel will never show would be a list that lies.

The two members a room may never lose

RefusedBecauseWhat to do instead
the last agent a room with none is about no project, and a room about no project is readable by nobody — including whoever just emptied it. put another agent in first, or delete the room.
the last person of a one-to-one room such a room is read by the people in it, so taking the last one out has exactly the same effect from the other side. add another person first, or delete the room.

Both refusals name the reason and the way out, because a refusal that says only "no" leaves the person holding a room they cannot fix.

What did not change

A room opened by an arriving message still opens, is still answered in, and still appears in the list exactly as before. This is a second way in and not a replacement: the channels open rooms the way they always have, and what is new is that a person can now open one deliberately and say who is in it before anybody says anything.
